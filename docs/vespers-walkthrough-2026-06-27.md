## The Service, Block by Block

### 1. Opening — Reader

`Blessed is our God…` → *O Heavenly King* → Trisagion → *O Most Holy Trinity* → Our Father → 12× Lord-have-mercy. Choir is silent. Standard reader's start to every service.

### 2. Psalm 103 — the *Proemial Psalm*

*"Bless the Lord, O my soul…"* — every Vespers begins here. It is the creation psalm; the service opens by setting evening within the arc of God's creation. On the greatest vigils, selected verses are sung by the choir as the *Anoixantaria*; most parishes (including Tyler) simply have the reader read it.

### 3. Great Litany ("Peace Litany") — Deacon & Choir

*"In peace let us pray to the Lord…"* Choir's first sung work: **"Lord, have mercy"** singly after each petition, **"To Thee, O Lord"** at the Theotokos commemoration, **"Amen"** at the exclamation.

### 4. Kathisma I — *"Blessed is the Man"*

Because this is **Great** Vespers, the first Kathisma of the Psalter is *sung*, not read. Selected verses of Psalms 1–3 with the choir responding **"Alleluia, alleluia, alleluia"** after each, ending with the Small Doxology and a triple Alleluia + "Glory to Thee, O God."

📚 **Kathisma** = one of the 20 sections the Psalter is divided into. Kathisma I = Psalms 1–8.

> Tyler sings the **Holy Transfiguration Monastery (Brookline, MA)** setting here, not the OCA Service Book default.

### 5. Little Litany — Deacon & Choir

A shortened version of the Great Litany. Same responses.

### 6. Lord, I Have Cried — *the heart of Vespers*

📚 **Vocabulary cashed out here**

- **Sticheron / stichera** — the hymns slotted between psalm verses.
- **Octoechos** — "Book of Eight Tones," the 8-week cycle of resurrectional hymns. The first 7 come from there.
- **Menaion** — the saints' calendar book, one volume per month. Cyrus & John's three stichera + the "Glory" Doxastikon come from June 28.
- **Doxastikon** — the hymn at "Glory to the Father…" From *doxa* = glory. Usually the saint's most important sticheron of the set.
- **Dogmatikon** — a *special* Theotokion sung at "Now and ever…" at Saturday Great Vespers Lord-I-Call. Each tone has one. It expounds the *dogma* of the Incarnation. Tonight: the Dogmatikon of Tone 3.

### 7. The Entrance & "O Gladsome Light"

Clergy process from the altar; the choir sings **"O Gladsome Light"** (*Phos Hilaron*) — likely the oldest extant Christian hymn outside Scripture (~3rd c.). This belongs to Great Vespers only; Daily Vespers skips the Entrance.

### 8. Evening Prokeimenon — Tone 6: *"The Lord is King…"*

📚 **Prokeimenon** = a versicle + verses sung before what *would* be a reading. On Saturday Great Vespers (any week, any tone) it is always *"The Lord is King, in beauty has He robed himself"* in **Tone 6**. Choir sings the refrain; deacon chants the verses.

> Tonight has **no OT readings**. Three paroemia (OT lessons) appear only on great-feast eves and polyeleos+ ranks. An ordinary Sunday eve has the prokeimenon alone.

### 9. Litany of Fervent Supplication ("Augmented Litany")

*"Let us say with all our soul…"* The choir responds **"Lord, have mercy"** singly, then **triply** ("Lord have mercy, Lord have mercy, Lord have mercy") after each of the petitions that follow. Hierarchs named: Metropolitan Tikhon (primate), Archbishop Alexander (ruling).

### 10. "Vouchsafe, O Lord" — Reader

*"Vouchsafe, O Lord, to keep us this night without sin…"* A short evening prayer of dependence on God. Choir rests.

### 11. Litany of Completion ("Litany of Supplication")

*"Let us complete our evening prayer…"* — note the choir's response **shifts** to **"Grant this, O Lord"** for most petitions. Ends with the bowing-heads prayer.

### 12. Aposticha — *stichera at the End*

📚 **Aposticha** = stichera sung at the *end* of Vespers, with specific psalm verses appended *after* each one. (The reverse pattern from Lord-I-Call, where the verse comes first.)

### 13. Nunc Dimittis + Trisagion + Our Father — Reader

*"Lord, now lettest Thou Thy servant depart in peace, according to Thy word…"* (St. Simeon's prayer from Luke 2.) Followed by Trisagion prayers and the Our Father. Choir rests.

📚 **Troparion** = a short summary hymn for a feast or saint. Each saint and each tone has one. The Dismissal Theotokion at the end of Vespers is the *resurrectional* one in the week's tone — Tone 3 tonight.

### 15. Dismissal — Priest & Choir

*"Wisdom" / "Father, bless" / "Blessed be He Who Is…"* → *"More honorable than the Cherubim…"* → the priest's final blessing → the conclusion through the Theotokos.

## Vocabulary glossary (alphabetical)

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Aposticha** | Stichera at the *end* of Vespers; psalm verses come *after* each. |
| **Dogmatikon** | Special Theotokion at Saturday Great Vespers LIC "Now"; expounds the dogma of the Incarnation. One per tone. |
| **Doxastikon** | The sticheron sung at "Glory to the Father…"; usually the saint's chief hymn. |
| **Idiomelon** | A sticheron with its own unique melody (not borrowed). Opposite: *prosomoion* (sung "to the tune of…"). |
| **Kathisma** | One of 20 sections of the Psalter. Kathisma I = Pss 1–8. |
| **Lord, I Have Cried (LIC)** | Pss 140, 141, 129, 116 with stichera between verses; the central hymnographic moment of Vespers. |
| **Menaion** | Fixed-calendar saints' book, one volume per month. Source for the day's commemoration. |
| **Octoechos** | "Eight Tones" book; 8-week cycle of resurrectional hymns. Source for Tone-3 hymns tonight. |
| **Paroemia** | Old Testament reading at Vespers; appears on great feasts. None tonight. |
| **Prokeimenon** | Versicle + verses before a reading (or stand-alone). Tonight: *"The Lord is King"* Tone 6. |
| **Sticheron / stichera** | The workhorse hymn form — short hymns slotted between psalm verses. |
| **Theotokion** | Any hymn in honor of the Theotokos. A set of stichera almost always ends with one at "Now and ever." |
| **Tone** | One of 8 melodic modes; rotates weekly; governs Octoechos selection. |
| **Troparion** | A short summary hymn for a feast or saint. Sung at the close of Vespers. |
| **Vespers (Great vs Daily)** | Great: Saturday eve, feast eves, vigils — has Entrance, sung Kathisma, possibly OT readings. Daily: weekday eves — no Entrance, Kathisma read, no readings. |
